# Positioning basis: values, strengths, audiences

**Status:** 6 October 2026. This is the basis for the design, not a design brief. It carries
only statements the owners have confirmed or that the company already publishes.

## Values

The company's value statement is **"Erfahrung. Klarheit. Begeisterung."**, in English
"Experience. Clarity. Enthusiasm."

Seven themes recur across the company's published writing:

1. **Proof instead of claims.** Work that can be inspected: "Expertise you can inspect"
   (Skills), "Prove, don't claim" (Ferramenta), "The repo shows the work" (Palamedes).
2. **Durability and craft.** Good architecture shows in how easy the next change is.
3. **Deliberately small and personally accountable.** "Zwei Architekten. Kein Dazwischen." No
   juniors, no hand-off to third parties.
4. **Judgment before hype.** Also for AI and new tools: the honest answer about when the
   simpler solution is the better one.
5. **Sharing knowledge.** Teams should be left stronger: pairing, reviews, and standards that
   remain when the consultants leave.
6. **Open source as gratitude.** "Open source shaped our careers. We give back." Open standards
   over proprietary formats.
7. **Calm and understatement.** Not loud, not trend-driven; trust through restraint.

## Audiences

| Site        | Audience                                                             | Primary goal       |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Consulting  | Technical decision makers; also intermediaries who pass on a profile | Book an intro call |
| Software    | Users and buyers of the products, partners, possible team members    | Discover a product |
| Open Source | Developers, technical evaluators, possible contributors              | Open a project     |
| Skills      | Developers and team leads who run coding agents                      | Install a skill    |

The Software audience splits by product: Terminaro addresses non-technical owners of small
businesses, Palamedes and Dalo address developers.

## Decisions by the owners

- **Two worlds.** Consulting was pulled out into its own site so that the products can be
  positioned cleanly on the Software side. Tone and wording may differ between the Software
  world and the Consulting world.
- **Market.** International first on every site, with the German roots noticeable and offers
  that also work in the DACH market.
- **Register.** Formal everywhere: "Sie" in German, business English in English.
- **Claims.** Qualitative and written for marketing, for example "one of the fastest Markdown
  parsers" or "built in modern Rust". No hand-maintained figures; live numbers come from the
  metrics service.
- **Consulting on the developer sites.** Open Source actively promotes Consulting; the Effective
  Agent site does so on its own.
- **Old sites.** The old Software site and the old Open Source site are outdated and carry
  little weight as references.

## Tone

- **German (Consulting):** short paired statements, differentiation by negation ("Keine
  Folien-Architektur, sondern Arbeit im Code"), English trade terms left as they are.
- **English (Open Source, Skills, products):** plain and factual, engineer to engineer, with
  explicit self-limitation ("Results still depend on the model").
- **Avoided:** buzzwords, stock photos, anonymous team pages, staffing-agency signals,
  invented metrics, and invented references.
